# Frameworks (v3). Syntax:
#  @@@ UNIVERSAL            then  nn: Title | what you say (or -) | why
#  @@ id | icon | Title | blurb       framework
#    step: key | !Title (! = non-negotiable) | say (or -) | why
#    nn: framework-level non-negotiable
#  @ id | Title | station ids | unit idxs      member (presentation / system / task)
#    use: when this applies    ch: stepkey | what changes    tree: Branch | leaf; leaf

@@@ UNIVERSAL
nn: Bare below the elbows; visible hand hygiene on entering and leaving | - | Official Nottingham anchor: "decontaminates hands when entering clinical area". Examiners tick what they see, so gel where they can see you.
nn: Introduce yourself with your full name and role | Hello, my name is Nana, I'm a third-year medical student. | First professionalism anchor. The actor is later asked whether they would want you as their doctor; warmth starts here.
nn: Identify the patient with two identifiers (name + date of birth), and check them against the chart, form or wristband | Can I check your full name and date of birth, please? | Official anchor: "correctly identifies patient using at least two patient identifiable details". In prescribing and procedure stations, check against the chart or form too.
nn: Explain why you are there and get consent before you start (again before any intimate or painful step) | I've been asked to ___. Is that OK with you? | No consent, no marks for what follows. Re-consenting before an intimate step is a separate mark.
nn: Ask about pain before you touch; offer a chaperone for breast, genital and rectal examinations | Before I start, are you sore anywhere? | Anchor: "checks if patient has any pain anywhere". Hurting the patient is a professionalism drop; a chaperone is a safety mark.
nn: Open question first, then listen without interrupting | What's brought you in today? | The golden minute. The actor's second sentence usually holds the cue you need.
nn: Ideas, concerns and expectations in every station, even A–E and prescribing | Is there anything in particular worrying you about this? | Communication 3 → 4 depends on finding and answering the hidden worry. Every actor brief has one.
nn: Plain English: explain every medical word; chunk and check | In simple terms, ___. Does that make sense so far? | Anchor: "avoids medical jargon" and "checks patient's understanding".
nn: Signpost each change of topic, then summarise before the plan | I'm going to ask some questions about your general health now. | Your formative feedback named structure. Signposting is the cheapest structure mark there is.
nn: Allergies and the reaction before any drug, fluid, dressing or plaster | Do you have any allergies? What happened when you took it? | Rash vs anaphylaxis changes the prescription. It is a critical item in every prescribing scheme.
nn: Say your reasoning aloud with the reasoning sentence | The important finding is ___. It suggests ___ because ___. The next step is ___. | Clinical reasoning is only marked if the examiner hears it. Your feedback named this sentence.
nn: Treat as you find in an unwell patient, and call for senior help early | I'd like to call for senior help now while I continue my assessment. | A–E anchors reward "treated things as soon as found" and "recognises need to escalate".
nn: Safety-net with specific warning signs, who to contact and how fast | If you notice ___, call 999 or come straight back. | A named safety item in almost every scheme; "come back if worse" alone scores less.
nn: Respect autonomy and dignity: no judgement about smoking, alcohol or drugs; cover the patient | It's your choice, and I'm here to help whichever way you decide. | Anchor: "respects patient's right for autonomy to continue to smoke" and "treats patient with dignity".
nn: Be honest about limits; never bluff | I'm not certain, so I'd check the BNF and with my senior. | Unsafe confident statements fail a station; honest checking never does.
nn: Watch the clock: by 8:00 stop gathering and give the plan | - | Your feedback: protect the last 2 minutes for management. The app turns amber at 8:00.
nn: Close: questions, leaflet, follow-up, thank, hand hygiene | Is there anything you'd like to ask me? I'll give you a leaflet as well. | Anchor: "gives opportunity for questions". A courteous close leaves the actor scoring you well.
nn: Talk to a manikin as if it were the patient | Mr Smith, I'm just going to listen to your chest. | Anchor: "addresses the manikin as the patient" and "communicates with the manikin".

@@ hx | 🗣 | History: any presenting complaint | One skeleton for every history station. Only the middle (the questions that split the differential) changes.
step: open | !Open the station | Hello, my name is Nana, I'm a third-year medical student. Can I check your full name and date of birth? I've been asked to find out what's brought you in today; is that OK? | Hand hygiene, name and role, two identifiers, purpose and consent: the professionalism marks are banked in the first 20 seconds.
step: pc | !Presenting complaint: open question, then the golden minute | What's brought you in today? ... Tell me more about that. | Patients give the key clue in their own words. Interrupting early loses information and the Communication mark.
step: hpc | Characterise the main symptom | When did it start? Was it sudden or gradual? Has it changed since? | SOCRATES for pain, a timeline for everything else. Timing sorts acute, subacute and chronic causes before you think of a diagnosis.
step: disc | Discriminators: the questions that split your differential | - | Information gathering is marked on questions that change your differential, not on a checklist read aloud. Run the tree for this complaint.
step: rf | !Red flags for this complaint | Have you noticed any weight loss, blood, or anything else that's worried you? | Missing the red flag is the commonest reason for a borderline fail. Every scheme has a red-flag line.
step: sr | Focused systems review | I'm going to ask a few quick questions about the rest of your health. | Catches the second problem the actor is briefed to reveal only if asked.
step: pmh | Past medical and surgical history | Do you have any other medical problems, or have you had any operations? | The past history often explains the presentation (previous gallstones, previous clot, diabetes).
step: dh | !Drugs, adherence and allergies | What medicines do you take, including anything you buy yourself? Any allergies, and what happened? | Drug causes are a favourite twist (NSAIDs, codeine, antipsychotics, warfarin). Allergy plus reaction is critical.
step: fh | Family history relevant to the complaint | Does anything run in the family? | Unlocks risk: sudden death under 40, early heart disease, bowel or breast cancer, clots.
step: sh | Social history: smoking, alcohol, drugs, work, home, function, driving | Do you smoke? How much alcohol do you drink in a typical week? Who's at home with you? | Quantify: pack-years and units. Function and driving feed straight into the plan.
step: ice | !Ideas, concerns, expectations and impact | What do you think might be going on? Is anything worrying you? What were you hoping we'd do today? | The actor's hidden worry is in every brief. Naming it moves Communication from 3 to 4.
step: sum | !Summarise and signpost | Let me check I've got this right: ___. Have I missed anything? | Shows structure (your feedback point) and gives the actor a chance to add the missing clue.
step: next | Present, explain or plan (what the door asks) | The important finding is ___. It suggests ___ because ___. The next step is ___. | Make the reasoning audible, then move to the plan by 8:00.
step: close | !Safety-net, questions, thank | If ___ happens, please call 999 or come straight back. Any questions? | Specific safety-netting is a safety item. Close warmly.
nn: Quantify smoking (pack-years) and alcohol (units per week) every time
nn: Ask about over-the-counter and herbal medicines, not just prescriptions
nn: Leave 2 minutes for the summary and plan

@ chest | Chest pain | PP05, PP06, PP07, PP20, PP106, PP108 | 0, 1, 2, 13
use: Any chest pain door: A&E, GP, ward, or "chest tightness".
ch: hpc | Full SOCRATES. Onset is the sorter: sudden and tearing = dissection; sudden and pleuritic = PE or pneumothorax; on exertion and relieved by rest = angina.
ch: disc | Exertion vs rest; radiation to jaw, arm or back; pleuritic (worse on breathing or lying flat, better sitting forward); positional or tender to touch; relation to food, acid brash.
ch: rf | Pain at rest for more than 15 minutes, sweating and vomiting, tearing pain to the back, collapse, haemoptysis, breathlessness.
ch: sr | Breathlessness, palpitations, syncope, calf swelling, cough and fever, reflux, weight loss.
ch: pmh | Previous MI, stents, CABG, diabetes, hypertension, high cholesterol, previous DVT or PE, recent surgery or long travel.
ch: dh | GTN use and effect, antiplatelets, anticoagulants, the contraceptive pill or HRT (clot risk), cocaine.
ch: fh | Heart disease under 60 in a first-degree relative; sudden death; clotting disorders.
ch: sh | Smoking pack-years, alcohol, cocaine, occupation (HGV and driving).
ch: next | ECG within 10 minutes, troponin; Wells if PE is possible.
tree: Cardiac | ACS (STEMI, NSTEMI, unstable angina); stable angina; pericarditis or myocarditis; aortic dissection
tree: Pulmonary | PE; pneumothorax; pneumonia or pleurisy
tree: Gastrointestinal | GORD or oesophagitis; oesophageal spasm or rupture; peptic ulcer; biliary colic; pancreatitis
tree: Chest wall and other | costochondritis or muscular; shingles before the rash; anxiety (a diagnosis of exclusion)
nn: Ask about radiation to the jaw, arm and back
nn: Cardiac risk factors: smoking, diabetes, hypertension, cholesterol, family history
pearl: Four killers first: ACS, dissection, PE, tension pneumothorax. Then the rest.

tree: Acute other | PE; aspiration; inhaled foreign body
tree: Chronic airways | asthma; COPD; bronchiectasis

@ palp | Palpitations, blackouts and falls | PP01, PP04, PP11, PP58 | 3, 96
use: "Palpitations", "collapse", "funny turn", "fall", "faint".
ch: hpc | Before, during, after. Before: what were they doing (standing, exertion, on standing up), any warning. During: witness account, how long out, colour, jerking. After: how fast they recovered, confusion, injury, long lie.
ch: disc | Palpitations: tap the rhythm (fast and regular = SVT; irregular = AF), onset and offset abrupt? Collapse: exertional syncope = AS or HCM; postural = orthostatic; prodrome of heat, nausea, greying = vasovagal; tongue-biting, incontinence, prolonged confusion = seizure.
ch: rf | Syncope on exertion or lying down, chest pain, family history of sudden death under 40, injury, new murmur.
ch: sr | Chest pain, breathlessness, weight loss and heat intolerance (thyroid), polyuria (diabetes), vision, focal weakness.
ch: pmh | Heart disease, valve disease, epilepsy, diabetes, previous falls (number and type), bone health, vision, frailty.
ch: dh | Antihypertensives, diuretics, alpha-blockers, sedatives, antipsychotics (QT), hypoglycaemics, recent changes.
ch: sh | Caffeine, alcohol, drugs, home (stairs), who's around (long lie), driving and occupation.
ch: next | ECG on every collapse; lying and standing BP; glucose. DVLA advice before they leave.
tree: Cardiac | arrhythmia (AF, SVT, VT, heart block, WPW); outflow obstruction (AS, HCM)
tree: Reflex | vasovagal; situational (cough, micturition)
tree: Orthostatic | drugs; volume depletion; autonomic failure (diabetes, Parkinson's)
tree: Not syncope | seizure; hypoglycaemia; mechanical trip; TIA (rarely)
nn: Witness account
nn: Driving: tell them what DVLA says before they leave
pearl: Before, during, after. Exertional syncope is cardiac until proven otherwise.

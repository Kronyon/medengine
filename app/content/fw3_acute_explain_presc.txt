@@ acute | 🚨 | Acute assessment: A to E | The same nine moves whatever the diagnosis. Treat as you find, reassess, escalate.
step: safe | !Danger, response, call for help, monitoring | Hello, can you hear me? I'm Nana, one of the doctors. I'd like to call for senior help and get monitoring on: sats, BP, ECG. | Anchors: "recognises need to escalate". Calling early is never wrong; calling late is.
step: a | Airway: look, listen, feel | Can you tell me your name? | Talking = patent. Stridor, gurgling or silence needs action now (suction, jaw thrust, adjunct, anaesthetist).
step: b | !Breathing: RR, sats, oxygen to target, trachea, expansion, percussion, auscultation; ABG, CXR | I'm giving 15 L oxygen via a non-rebreather mask, aiming for 94–98% (88–92% if he retains CO2). | Oxygen with a target range, then the findings that split asthma, pneumothorax, LVF and pneumonia.
step: c | !Circulation: HR, BP, CRT, JVP, heart sounds; 2 large-bore cannulas and bloods; fluids; ECG; catheter | Two large-bore cannulas: FBC, U&E, LFT, CRP, clotting, group and save, blood cultures, lactate, VBG. 500 mL crystalloid over 15 minutes, then reassess. | Specific bloods and a fluid bolus with a reassessment are named items in every A–E scheme.
step: d | !Disability: AVPU or GCS, pupils, glucose, temperature, drug chart | What's his capillary glucose? Can I see the drug chart? | Glucose in D is never optional. The drug chart finds the cause (opioids, insulin, anticoagulants).
step: e | Exposure: abdomen, calves, rashes, bleeding, wounds, drains, catheter bag; dignity | I'm going to look at his tummy and legs, keeping him covered. | The hidden finding is often in E: a bleeding wound, a swollen calf, a full bladder, a rash.
step: re | !Reassess after every intervention | Let me reassess his airway and breathing after the oxygen. | Anchor: "reassessed" and "offers to re-assess patient following an intervention".
step: sbar | !Escalate with SBAR | Situation: I'm Nana, the FY1 on ward 3, calling about ___ who has ___. Background: ___. Assessment: ___. Recommendation: please could you review him within ___ minutes. | SBAR handover is often a whole domain. Ask for something specific and a time.
step: dx | Working diagnosis and definitive treatment | The most likely diagnosis is ___ because ___. | Name the diagnosis, the treatment and where they should go (CCU, HDU, theatre, ITU).
nn: Call for help early
nn: Oxygen with a target range
nn: Glucose in D
nn: Reassess after every intervention

@ acs | Chest pain: ACS | PP07, PP06, PP108 | 0, 2
use: Central crushing chest pain in A&E or on the ward.
ch: b | Oxygen only if sats below 94% (not routinely).
ch: c | 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes; troponin; 2 cannulas; careful fluids if hypotensive (cardiogenic shock: 250 mL boluses with senior input).
ch: dx | STEMI: aspirin 300 mg, GTN, IV morphine with an antiemetic if pain persists, call the cath lab for primary PCI (within 120 minutes of first contact) or fibrinolysis. NSTEMI: aspirin 300 mg, antithrombin (fondaparinux), GRACE score, ticagrelor or prasugrel per plan, angiography within 72 hours if intermediate or high risk.
tree: ST elevation | anterior (V1–V4, LAD); inferior (II, III, aVF, RCA); lateral (I, aVL, V5–V6, circumflex); posterior (ST depression V1–V3 + tall R)
tree: Mimics | aortic dissection (unequal arm BPs, back pain); pericarditis (saddle ST, PR depression); PE
nn: ECG within 10 minutes
pearl: Don't wait for troponin in a STEMI.

@ breath | Acute breathlessness | PP16, PP97, PP101 | 6, 11, 13, 15, 16, 19
use: A–E of the breathless patient (asthma, COPD, PE, pneumothorax, anaphylaxis, LVF).
ch: b | The B findings split it: wheeze (asthma, COPD, anaphylaxis), silent chest (life-threatening asthma), unilateral hyper-resonance with tracheal deviation (tension pneumothorax), bibasal crackles (LVF), clear chest with hypoxia (PE).
ch: c | Pink frothy sputum, raised JVP and oedema (LVF); swollen calf (PE); hypotension with rash or stridor (anaphylaxis).
ch: dx | Asthma: salbutamol 5 mg + ipratropium 500 micrograms nebulised (oxygen-driven), prednisolone 40–50 mg, magnesium IV if severe. COPD: target 88–92%, nebs, prednisolone 30 mg 5 days, antibiotics if purulent, NIV if pH <7.35 and PaCO2 >6.5 after treatment. Tension pneumothorax: needle decompression, then a chest drain. PE: anticoagulate, CTPA. Anaphylaxis: IM adrenaline 500 micrograms (0.5 mL of 1 mg/mL) anterolateral thigh, repeat at 5 minutes. LVF: sit up, oxygen, IV furosemide, stop IV fluids, CPAP if needed.
tree: Wheeze | asthma; COPD; anaphylaxis
tree: Crackles | LVF; pneumonia
tree: Clear chest, hypoxic | PE; early pneumonia
tree: Asymmetry | pneumothorax; effusion; collapse
nn: Target sats (88–92% if CO2 retainer)
pearl: Silent chest, cyanosis, poor effort, exhaustion = life-threatening asthma: get an anaesthetist.

@ sepsis | Sepsis and the unwell febrile patient | PP99, PP100, PP97 | 46, 8, 19, 42
use: Fever, hypotension, NEWS2 high, post-op temperature.
ch: c | Sepsis Six within an hour: oxygen to target, blood cultures, IV antibiotics (check allergy and reaction), IV fluids, lactate, urine output.
ch: e | Look for the source: chest, urine, abdomen, wounds, lines, joints, skin, new murmur.
ch: dx | Source control and senior review; lactate over 4 or hypotension after fluids = critical care.
tree: Sources | chest; urine; abdomen (cholangitis, perforation, anastomotic leak); skin and soft tissue; lines and valves (endocarditis); meninges
nn: Blood cultures before antibiotics, without delaying antibiotics
pearl: Lactate, cultures, antibiotics, fluids, oxygen, urine: in the first hour.

@ bleed | Haemorrhage: GI bleed, post-op bleed | PP29, PP97 | 32, 38, 45
use: Haematemesis, melaena, post-op shock.
ch: c | Two large-bore cannulas, crossmatch, FBC, clotting, fibrinogen; major haemorrhage protocol if unstable; O-negative blood while waiting; correct anticoagulation (vitamin K and PCC for warfarin).
ch: dx | Upper GI: Glasgow-Blatchford score; endoscopy within 24 hours (immediately after resuscitation if unstable); suspected varices: terlipressin + prophylactic antibiotics before endoscopy. Post-op: back to theatre.
tree: Shock class | tachycardia first; then narrowing pulse pressure; hypotension is late
nn: Crossmatch and senior help early
pearl: Hypotension is a late sign of bleeding; young people compensate until they crash.

@ metab | Diabetes emergencies: DKA, HHS, hypoglycaemia | PP48, PP52, PP47 | 68, 69, 70
use: Unwell diabetic, vomiting, drowsy, low glucose.
ch: d | Glucose and ketones in D; Kussmaul breathing and pear-drop breath.
ch: dx | DKA (glucose >11 or known diabetes, ketones ≥3, pH <7.3 or bicarbonate <15): 0.9% saline (1 L over 1 hour then slower), fixed-rate insulin 0.1 units/kg/hour, potassium per level, continue long-acting insulin, add 10% glucose when glucose <14. Under 18: paediatric (BSPED) protocol. Hypoglycaemia: conscious = 15–20 g fast sugar; drowsy = IV 10% glucose 150–200 mL (or glucagon 1 mg IM).
tree: DKA vs HHS | DKA: ketones high, acidotic, young, hours; HHS: osmolality >320, glucose >30, no significant ketosis, older, days
nn: Check ketones and potassium
pearl: Fluids first, then insulin; potassium keeps them alive.

@ tox | Overdose and poisoning | PP52, PP68 | 111
use: Collapsed or drowsy after an overdose.
ch: d | Pupils (pinpoint = opioid; dilated = TCA, sympathomimetic), glucose, GCS.
ch: c | ECG: QRS over 100 ms in TCA overdose.
ch: dx | Opioid: naloxone 400 micrograms IV, titrate. TCA: sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 50 mL IV if QRS broad, arrhythmia or acidosis; ICU. Paracetamol: level at 4 hours or more, acetylcysteine if above the treatment line (or staggered, or unknown timing). Check paracetamol and salicylate levels in every overdose. Toxbase.
nn: Paracetamol and salicylate levels in every overdose
nn: Psychiatric assessment once medically fit
pearl: Pinpoint = opioids; wide QRS + dilated pupils = TCA.

@ postop | Post-operative deterioration | PP97, PP103, PP105 | 46, 45, 74
use: Called to a post-op patient: breathless, agitated, hypotensive, in pain.
ch: safe | Read the op note: what operation, when, any complications.
ch: e | Wound, drains, abdomen (distension, peritonism), calves, catheter and fluid balance, neck after thyroidectomy.
ch: dx | Day 0–1: bleeding, atelectasis, hypocalcaemia or haematoma after thyroid surgery. Day 2–3: fluid overload (LVF), pneumonia. Day 5–7: anastomotic leak, wound infection. Day 7–10+: DVT and PE.
tree: Time after surgery | bleed (hours); atelectasis (day 1–2); fluid overload (day 2–3); leak or sepsis (day 5–7); VTE (day 7–10)
nn: Check the fluid chart and drains
pearl: Think in time: bleed, collapse, fluid, leak, clot.

@ arrest | Cardiac arrest: ALS | PP98, PP52 | 3
use: "The patient collapses", "manage the arrest".
ch: safe | Danger, response, shout for help and the arrest team, open the airway, look, listen and feel for 10 s (and a central pulse).
ch: b | CPR 30:2, 100–120 per minute, 5–6 cm deep; bag-valve-mask with oxygen.
ch: c | Pads on, pause for rhythm check (no more than 5 s). Shockable (VF, pulseless VT): shock, then 2 minutes CPR; adrenaline 1 mg and amiodarone 300 mg after the 3rd shock; adrenaline every 3–5 minutes. Non-shockable (PEA, asystole): adrenaline 1 mg as soon as access, 2 minutes CPR between checks.
ch: dx | Reversible causes: 4 Hs (hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo or hyperkalaemia and metabolic, hypothermia) and 4 Ts (thrombosis, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins). ROSC: A–E, 12-lead ECG, aim sats 94–98%.
tree: Shockable | VF; pulseless VT
tree: Non-shockable | PEA; asystole
nn: Minimise pauses in compressions
pearl: Shock, 2 minutes; after the 3rd: adrenaline + amiodarone.

@@ expl | 💬 | Explain, counsel, consent or break bad news | Every information-giving station is the same conversation: start where they are, chunk and check, share the plan, teach back, safety-net.
step: prep | Prepare: right person, private, notes and results read | - | Talking to a relative? Check who they are and that the patient is happy for you to share (or that it's in their best interests).
step: open | !Open: introduce, identify, purpose, permission | I've come to talk about your results / the treatment we're suggesting. Is now a good time? | Purpose and permission set the agenda; examiners mark an explicit agenda.
step: know | !Start where they are | What do you understand about what's been going on so far? | Anchor: "assesses patient's prior knowledge". Skipping it is the commonest Communication drop.
step: shot | Signpost (and give a warning shot for bad news) | I'd like to explain what the tests show, then talk about what we can do. Is that OK? / I'm afraid the results are not what we hoped. | Signposting structure was named in your feedback. A warning shot is a separate BBN mark.
step: c1 | !Chunk 1: what it is, in plain words | Normally ___. In your case, ___. It's a bit like ___. | Anchor structure: normal anatomy and physiology → what's gone wrong → cause. An analogy earns the plain-language mark.
step: c2 | Chunk 2: why it matters and what causes it | - | Complications and causes give the reason to act on the plan.
step: c3 | !Chunk 3: what we do: options with benefits, risks, alternatives (including doing nothing) | - | Shared decision-making and BRAN (benefits, risks, alternatives, nothing) are what Management and Consent marks look for.
step: chk | !Check after each chunk | Does that make sense so far? What questions do you have? | Chunk and check is in every information-giving anchor.
step: ice | !ICE and emotion: name it, normalise, support | It sounds like this has been really worrying. What's on your mind most? | Responding to emotion (and to the planted question) moves Communication to 4.
step: plan | Agree the plan, follow-up and who to contact | - | A shared plan with a follow-up date is the Management mark.
step: tb | !Teach-back summary | Just so I know I've explained it well, how would you explain this to your family? | Teach-back proves understanding; examiners love it.
step: sn | !Safety-net, leaflet, questions, thank | If you get ___, call 999. Here's a leaflet and the specialist nurse number. | Safety-netting and offering written information are named items.
nn: Ask what they already know
nn: Chunk and check
nn: Teach-back and safety-net

@ dx | Explain a diagnosis | PP32, PP49, PP50, PP58, PP106, PP107 | 29, 67, 2, 73, 96

@ drug | Medication counselling | PP03, PP39, PP63, PP73, PP106 | 3, 72, 103, 138, 2

@ consent | Consent for a procedure | PP27, PP29, PP37, PP85, PP96 | 48, 32, 75, 87

@@ presc | 💊 | Prescribing on a drug chart | Every prescription is checked the same way. Only the drug choice changes.
step: pt | !Patient details on every page: name, DOB, hospital number, weight, ward, date | - | Anchor: "correctly identifies patient" and "allergy and patient info recorded". Weight matters for doses (gentamicin, aminophylline, LMWH).
step: allergy | !Allergy box: drug and reaction, signed and dated (or NKDA) | Do you have any allergies to medicines? What happened? | Prescribing before completing the allergy box is a fail point in Nottingham schemes.
step: check | Check: renal and liver function, pregnancy, current drugs and interactions (BNF) | - | Interactions and renal dosing are where the examiner's planted twist sits (eGFR, warfarin, citalopram).
step: choose | Choose the drug per the guideline provided | - | Use the local guideline in the station; off-guideline is accepted only if clinically appropriate.
step: write | !Write it: approved name in capitals, dose, route, frequency, start date, indication | - | Units and micrograms in full; no trailing zeros; legible.
step: section | Right section: once-only, regular, as-required, infusion, oxygen, anticoagulant | - | Wrong section is a named error (e.g. a stat dose in regular; warfarin outside its chart).
step: prn | As-required: indication, minimum interval, maximum in 24 hours | - | Missing a maximum dose is the commonest PRN error.
step: stop | Stop or suspend: line through, date, sign, reason | - | Sick-day rules: suspend ACE inhibitors, diuretics, metformin, NSAIDs, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors when acutely unwell.
step: sign | !Sign, print name and bleep, date every entry | - | An unsigned prescription is not a prescription.
step: review | Antibiotics: indication and review or stop date; VTE assessment; avoid double anticoagulation | - | Cross off enoxaparin when starting therapeutic anticoagulation.
step: tell | Tell the patient and plan monitoring | - | Counselling or a monitoring plan (U&E, INR, levels) completes the station.
nn: Allergy box before any drug
nn: Sign and date every entry
nn: PRN maximum dose

pearl: Oral morphine to SC: halve it. Breakthrough: a sixth.
